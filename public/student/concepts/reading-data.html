SAT MATH · CHAPTER 23 REV. 01

Reading Data

Bar Charts · Line Graphs · Scatterplots

This chapter uses no new algebra — it's a chart-reading skill set. Every rule below comes with a small chart of its own, so the skill and the picture are learned together. Formulas first — including two SAT shortcuts not spelled out in this chapter — then worked examples, and finally practice questions from easy to extreme.

01 Formulas & Skills
Reading a bar chart
Match the bar's height to the y-axis
Taller bar = larger value. Compare bars directly, no formula needed.
Reading a line graph
Go up from x, then across to the y-axis
If the point falls between grid lines, estimate by interpolating
Average rate of change
\(\text{rate}=\dfrac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}=\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}\)
It's just slope — between any two points read off the graph
Greatest / least rate of change
Steepest segment = greatest rate; flattest = least
Scan by eye first — you rarely need to compute every segment
Reading a scatterplot
Each dot \(=\) one \((x,y)\) data point
Read its position against both axes to get the pair of values
Correlation
Positive ↗, negative ↘, or no pattern
Describes the overall trend of the scattered points
Line of best fit
A straight line that best represents the trend
Used to estimate values — it won't pass through every point
Comparing two data values
\(\dfrac{\text{value A}}{\text{value B}}\times100\%\)
"A is what percent/fraction of B" — read both values, then divide
Doubling / halving time
Find where \(y=2y_0\), read the x-values, subtract
Doubling time \(=x_2-x_1\), same idea for halving
Combine chart data with a given rate
\(\text{total}=(\text{quantity from chart})\times(\text{rate given})\)
Revenue \(=\) units sold (bar chart) \(\times\) price (given in the problem)
SAT shortcut — not spelled out in this chapter
Interpolation vs. extrapolation
Inside the plotted range = safer; beyond it = riskier
Predicting past the last data point assumes the trend keeps going
SAT shortcut — connects to the Statistics chapters
