Angles

Triangles · Parallel Lines · Polygons

Every formula below comes with its own diagram, so the rule and the picture are learned together. Formulas first — including two SAT shortcuts not spelled out in this chapter — then worked examples, and finally practice questions from easy to extreme.

01 Formulas
Angles on a line
\(a+b=180°\)
Angles on a straight line always sum to 180°
Angles around a point
\(a+b+c=360°\)
All angles around a single point sum to 360°
Vertical angles
\(\angle1=\angle3,\ \ \angle2=\angle4\)
Angles directly across from each other are congruent
Triangle angle sum
\(a+b+c=180°\)
The three interior angles of any triangle sum to 180°
Exterior angle theorem
\(x=a+b\)
An exterior angle equals the sum of the two remote interior angles
Parallel lines & a transversal
\(\angle3=\angle5,\ \ \angle4=\angle6\)
Alternate interior angles (inside the parallel lines, opposite sides of the transversal) are congruent
Alternate exterior angles
\(\angle1=\angle7,\ \ \angle2=\angle8\)
Alternate exterior angles (outside the parallel lines, opposite sides) are congruent
Corresponding angles
\(\angle1=\angle5,\ \ \angle2=\angle6,\ \ \angle3=\angle7,\ \ \angle4=\angle8\)
Same relative position at each intersection — congruent
Same-side interior angles
\(\angle4+\angle5=180°,\ \ \angle3+\angle6=180°\)
Interior angles on the same side of the transversal are supplementary, not congruent
Polygon interior angle sum
\(\text{sum}=180(n-2)\)
A pentagon (n=5) splits into 3 triangles: \(3\times180°=540°\)
Exterior angles of any polygon
\(\text{sum of exterior angles}=360°\)
True for every convex polygon, no matter how many sides
SAT shortcut — not spelled out in this chapter
Each interior/exterior angle (regular polygon)
\(\text{interior}=\dfrac{180(n-2)}{n},\quad \text{exterior}=\dfrac{360}{n}\)
Regular hexagon (n=6): interior \(=120°\), exterior \(=60°\)
SAT shortcut — the exterior-angle version isn't in this chapter
