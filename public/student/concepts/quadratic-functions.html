Quadratic Functions

Standard Form · Vertex · Discriminant

Parabolas — the "U"-shaped graphs of quadratic functions. How to read standard form, intercept form, and vertex form; how to find the vertex without graphing; and how the discriminant instantly tells you the number of real solutions. Diagrams and rules first, then worked examples and a 10-question practice set (3 easy · 3 medium · 3 hard · 1 extreme).

01 Techniques
the amber dot marks the vertex — always the min (opens up) or max (opens down)
the white dots are where the parabola crosses the x-axis — that's exactly what "real roots" means
Standard form & opening direction
\(y=ax^2+bx+c\)
\(a>0\) → opens up (min); \(a<0\) → opens down (max)
The y-intercept is hiding in plain sight
\(\text{y-intercept} = (0, c)\)
\(y=x^2-4x-21\) → y-intercept \((0,-21)\)
Intercept form shows the roots directly
\(y=a(x-p)(x-q)\)
\(y=(x+3)(x-7)\) has roots \(-3\) and \(7\)
Sum and product of the roots
\(\text{sum}=-\dfrac{b}{a}, \quad \text{product}=\dfrac{c}{a}\)
\(x^2-4x-21\): sum \(=4\), product \(=-21\)
Vertex x-coordinate = average of the roots
\(x_{vertex}=\dfrac{p+q}{2}=-\dfrac{b}{2a}\)
roots \(-3,7\) → \(x_{vertex}=\dfrac{-3+7}{2}=2\)
Vertex form reveals the vertex instantly
\(y=a(x-h)^2+k, \ \text{vertex}=(h,k)\)
\(y=(x-2)^2-25\) has vertex \((2,-25)\)
Watch the sign inside vertex form
\(y=a(x-h)^2+k \Rightarrow \text{vertex is } (h,k), \text{ not } (-h,k)\)
\(y=-2(x+5)^2+8\) → vertex is \((-5,8)\)
The discriminant counts the real solutions
\(D=b^2-4ac\)
\(D>0\!:\)two, \(D=0\!:\)one, \(D<0\!:\)zero real solutions
Completing the square (standard → vertex form)
\(ax^2+bx+c = a\left(x+\dfrac{b}{2a}\right)^2+\left(c-\dfrac{b^2}{4a}\right)\)
\(x^2+6x+5=(x+3)^2-4\)
Line tangent to a parabola
\(\text{set line}=\text{parabola} \Rightarrow D=0 \text{ for exactly one intersection}\)
tangent when the combined quadratic has \(D=0\)
