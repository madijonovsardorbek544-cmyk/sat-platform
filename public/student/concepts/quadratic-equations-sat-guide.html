SAT MATH · CHAPTER 12 REV. 01

Quadratic Equations

Rules · Techniques · Practice

Every quadratic question boils down to the same question: what's the fastest legal way to get from the equation to the answer? Learn the ten core rules, use the decision guide to pick the right technique instantly, work through six full solutions, then climb the ten-question ladder: three easy, three medium, three hard, and one true extreme.

01 Formulas
Core Quadratic Toolkit
Standard form
\(ax^2+bx+c=0\)
\(2x^2-5x-8=0\): \(a{=}2,\ b{=}{-}5,\ c{=}{-}8\)
Zero Product Property
\((x-r_1)(x-r_2)=0 \Rightarrow x=r_1 \text{ or } r_2\)
\((x+7)(x-2)=0 \Rightarrow x=-7,2\)
The quadratic formula
\(x=\dfrac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}\)
\(x^2{+}5x{-}14=0 \Rightarrow x=\dfrac{-5\pm9}{2}\)
Sum of the solutions
\(r_1+r_2 = -\dfrac{b}{a}\)
\(x^2{+}5x{-}14=0 \Rightarrow \text{sum}=-5\)
Product of the solutions
\(r_1\cdot r_2 = \dfrac{c}{a}\)
\(2x^2{-}5x{-}8=0 \Rightarrow \text{product}=-4\)
Factor out the GCF first
\(2x^3{+}42x^2{+}216x = 2x(x{+}9)(x{+}12)\)
always pull common factors out before factoring the trinomial
x-intercept → factor
x-intercept at \(n \Rightarrow (x-n)\) is a factor
intercepts at \(-9,-12,0 \Rightarrow (x{+}9)(x{+}12)x\)
Fractional intercept → cleared factor
intercept \(\dfrac{p}{q} \Rightarrow (qx-p)\), not \(\left(x-\dfrac{p}{q}\right)\)
intercept \(\dfrac12 \Rightarrow (2x-1)\)
u-substitution
let \(A=\) the repeated expression
\((x{+}1)^2{+}5(x{+}1){-}24{=}0 \Rightarrow A{=}x{+}1\)
Radical equations → quadratics
isolate the root, square, solve, then check
\(\sqrt{x+5}+1=x\)

SAT Bonus — Beyond This Chapter
The discriminant isn't introduced until a later chapter on quadratic functions, but questions about it show up on quadratic-equation problems constantly.
Which Technique Should You Use?
Two nice integer factors jump out?Factor & use the Zero Product Property
Ugly numbers, doesn't factor cleanly?Use the Quadratic Formula
Only asked for the sum or product of the roots?Skip solving — use \(-b/a\) or \(c/a\)
A repeated expression like \((x{+}1)\) appears twice?u-substitution
⚠ A square root is present?Isolate the radical BEFORE squaring — then check every solution in the original equation
The discriminant
\(b^2-4ac\)
\(>0\): two real roots · \(=0\): one root · \(<0\): no real roots
Plug in a known point
graph passes through \((m,n) \Rightarrow n = am^2+bm+c\)
substitute directly, then solve for the unknown constant
Vieta's shortcut with constants
use \(-b/a\) and \(c/a\) even when \(b,c\) contain unknowns
never have to actually solve for \(x\)
Match coefficients, don't expand blindly
compare corresponding terms directly
faster than multiplying out both sides
