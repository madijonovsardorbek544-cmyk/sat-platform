Statistics I

Mean · Median · Mode · Standard Deviation · Reading Data Displays

The center of a data set (mean, median, mode), how spread out it is (range and standard deviation — conceptual, never hand-calculated on the SAT), and how to read the same information off frequency tables, dot plots, box plots, and histograms. Diagrams and rules first, then worked examples and a 10-question practice set (3 easy · 3 medium · 3 hard · 1 extreme).

01 Techniques
stacked dots = frequency · mean = 4 · median = 3.5 · mode = 2 · range = 5
whiskers reach the min and max · the box spans Q1 to Q3 · the line inside is the median
Mean (average)
\(\text{mean}=\dfrac{\text{sum of values}}{\text{number of values}}\)
\(\{5,6,2,2,2,7\} \to \dfrac{24}{6}=4\)
Median
\(\text{the middle value once data is ordered}\)
\(\{2,2,2,5,6,7\} \to \dfrac{2+5}{2}=3.5\)
Mode
\(\text{the value that appears most often}\)
\(\{2,2,2,5,6,7\} \to \text{mode}=2\)
Range
\(\text{range}=\text{max}-\text{min}\)
\(\{2,2,2,5,6,7\} \to 7-2=5\)
Standard deviation — spread from the mean (conceptual only)
\(\text{closer to the mean} \to \text{smaller SD}; \ \text{more spread out} \to \text{bigger SD}\)
you will never calculate SD by hand on the SAT — only compare it
Adding a value pulls the mean toward it
\(\text{new mean}=\dfrac{\text{old sum}+\text{new value}}{\text{old count}+1}\)
a much heavier panda joins → the average weight rises
Frequency table: frequency = how many times a value occurs
\(\text{frequency}=\text{count of a value in the data set}\)
read the table like a compressed list of the data
Dot plot: stack height = frequency
\(\text{number of dots above a value} = \text{its frequency}\)
the tallest stack of dots is the mode
Box plot: the five-number summary
\(\text{min},\ Q_1 \text{ (25th \%ile)},\ \text{median (50th \%ile)},\ Q_3 \text{ (75th \%ile)},\ \text{max}\)
the box itself (Q1 to Q3) always holds the middle 50%
Histogram: bars group data into intervals
\(\text{bar height}=\text{frequency within that interval}\)
the sum of all bar heights = total number of data points
Outliers barely move the median but can swing the mean
\(\text{median: resistant to outliers} \quad \text{mean: sensitive to outliers}\)
replacing the max with an even bigger value changes the mean, not the median
