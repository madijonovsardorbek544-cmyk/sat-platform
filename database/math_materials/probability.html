01 Formulas
Basic probability
\(P = \dfrac{\text{number of target outcomes}}{\text{number of total possible outcomes}}\)
the foundation every other rule builds on
Table probability
\(P = \dfrac{\text{target count within the group}}{\text{count of the group being selected from}}\)
always identify the exact group you're picking from first
"Given that" = conditional probability
narrows the group you select from
everything after "given that" becomes your new, smaller total
"Or" from a table
add the two target counts together first
P(A or B) = (count A + count B) ÷ total
Complement rule
\(P(\text{not } X) = 1 - P(X)\)
useful whenever a question asks for "not," "neither," or "other than"
Geometric probability
\(P = \dfrac{\text{area of target region}}{\text{total area}}\)
points, darts, random locations — use area (or length) instead of counting
Order matters in "given that"
\(P(A \text{ given } B) \neq P(B \text{ given } A)\)
always select FROM the group named right after "given that"
Combined categories (variables)
P(A or B) = \(\dfrac{\text{count A} + \text{count B}}{\text{total}}\)
works the same way even when counts are unknown letters, like g and r
Backtracking from a known probability
probability × total = target count
rearrange the basic formula to solve for a missing piece
Comparing two probabilities
compute each one fully first
then subtract or compare the actual decimals/fractions — never guess from the raw numbers
Unequal group sizes
assign a variable to the smaller group
"twice as many" → let one group be x and the other 2x
WATCH OUT — "The probability that a student is in Club A, given that they're in Club B" and "the probability that a student is in Club B, given that they're in Club A" are almost always different numbers. The phrase right after "given that" tells you which group is your new total — read it carefully.
