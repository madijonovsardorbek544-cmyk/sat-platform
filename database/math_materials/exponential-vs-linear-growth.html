01 Formulas
Exponential model
\(y=a\cdot b^t\)
\(a=\)initial value, \(b=\)growth/decay factor
Generalized model (rate over k)
\(y=a\cdot b^{\,t/k}\)
\(k=\)time for one factor-\(b\) change; \(t,k\) same units
Compound interest (special case)
\(A=P(1+r)^t\)
\(3\%\) annual \(\to A=P(1.03)^t\)
Percent increase → growth factor
\(b=1+\dfrac{r}{100}\)
\(+8\% \to b=1.08\)
Percent decrease → decay factor
\(b=1-\dfrac{r}{100}\)
\(-25\% \to b=0.75\)
Growth vs. decay condition
\(b>1:\text{growth}\qquad 0 < b < 1:\text{decay}\)
\(b=1\) means no change at all
Linear growth/decay
\(y=mt+b\)
\(+5/\text{month} \to y=5t+100\)
Growth & decay graph shapes
\(\text{Growth: curves up, steeper over time}\)
\(\text{Decay: curves down, flattens toward } 0\)
Spotting it in a table
\(\text{constant ratio} \to \text{exponential}\)
constant difference \(\to\) linear
SAT shortcut — not spelled out in this chapter
Reading percent change from b
\(r\%=|b-1|\times100\)
\(b=1.15 \to 15\%\) increase; \(b=0.9\to10\%\) decrease
SAT shortcut — reverses Rules 04–05
