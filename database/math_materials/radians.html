Radians

Degree ⇄ Radian Conversion · Arc Length & Sector Area in Radians

A radian is just another unit for measuring angles — like using meters instead of feet. The one relationship π radians = 180° unlocks every conversion, and it makes the arc length and sector area formulas dramatically simpler. Diagram and rules first, then worked examples and a 10-question practice set (3 easy · 3 medium · 3 hard · 1 extreme).

01 Techniques

The one relationship everything is built on
\(\pi \text{ radians} = 180°\)
this is the "master key" — memorize this one line
Degrees → radians
\(\text{degrees}\times \dfrac{\pi \text{ rad}}{180°}=\text{radians}\)
\(45° \times \dfrac{\pi}{180}=\dfrac{\pi}{4}\) radians
Radians → degrees
\(\text{radians}\times \dfrac{180°}{\pi \text{ rad}}=\text{degrees}\)
\(2\times \dfrac{180}{\pi}=\dfrac{360}{\pi}\approx114.6°\)
Arc length is simpler in radians
\(L=r\theta \quad (\theta \text{ in radians})\)
\(r=6,\ \theta=\tfrac{\pi}{3} \to L=6\times\tfrac{\pi}{3}=2\pi\)
Sector area is simpler in radians
\(A=\dfrac12 r^2\theta \quad (\theta \text{ in radians})\)
\(r=6,\ \theta=\tfrac{\pi}{3} \to A=\tfrac12(36)\tfrac{\pi}{3}=6\pi\)
Recognize standard angles from a slope
\(\text{slope}=\sqrt3 \to 60°=\tfrac{\pi}{3};\ \ \text{slope}=1\to45°=\tfrac{\pi}{4}\)
slope \(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\to 30°=\tfrac{\pi}{6}\)
Common angle pairs worth memorizing
\(30°\!=\!\tfrac{\pi}{6},\ 45°\!=\!\tfrac{\pi}{4},\ 60°\!=\!\tfrac{\pi}{3},\ 90°\!=\!\tfrac{\pi}{2}\)
\(180°=\pi, \quad 360°=2\pi\)
