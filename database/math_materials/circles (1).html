01 Techniques
both the arc and the sector are the same fraction (θ⁄360) of the full circle
central ∠AOB (amber) = 80° · inscribed ∠ACB (violet), same arc AB = 40°, exactly half
left: a tangent line always meets the radius at a right angle · right: standard form (x−h)² + (y−k)² = r²
Circle basics
\(d=2r, \quad A=\pi r^2, \quad C=2\pi r\)
\(r=5 \to d=10,\ A=25\pi,\ C=10\pi\)
Arc length is a fraction of the circumference
\(L=\dfrac{\theta}{360}\times 2\pi r\)
\(\theta=60°, r=9 \to L=\dfrac{60}{360}(18\pi)=3\pi\)
Sector area is the same fraction of the total area
\(A_{sector}=\dfrac{\theta}{360}\times \pi r^2\)
\(\theta=90°, r=6 \to A=\dfrac{90}{360}(36\pi)=9\pi\)
Inscribed angle = half the central angle (same arc)
\(\angle_{inscribed}=\tfrac12\angle_{central}=\tfrac12\,\text{arc}\)
central 80° → inscribed 40°
An angle inscribed in a semicircle is always 90°
\(\text{diameter as one side} \Rightarrow \angle=90°\)
any triangle using a diameter as its hypotenuse is a right triangle
A radius to a tangent point is perpendicular to the tangent
\(\text{radius} \perp \text{tangent line, at the point of tangency}\)
great for setting up right-triangle Pythagorean problems
Tangent segments from the same external point are equal
\(PA=PB\) (tangents from external point P)
two tangent lines drawn from one outside point are congruent
A radius that bisects a chord is perpendicular to it
\(\text{radius bisects chord} \iff \text{radius} \perp \text{chord}\)
creates a right triangle: radius, half-chord, and distance to center
Equation of a circle (standard form)
\((x-h)^2+(y-k)^2=r^2\)
\((x+6)^2+(y-5)^2=64\) → center \((-6,5)\), \(r=8\)
Completing the square (to reach standard form)
\(x^2+bx \to \left(x+\tfrac{b}{2}\right)^2-\left(\tfrac{b}{2}\right)^2\)
\(x^2+6x+2 \to (x+3)^2-9+2=(x+3)^2-7\)
Circle from diameter endpoints
\(\text{center}=\left(\dfrac{x_1+x_2}{2},\dfrac{y_1+y_2}{2}\right), \ r=\dfrac{\text{diameter}}{2}\)
endpoints \((-5,-2),(9,-2)\) → center \((2,-2)\), \(r=7\)
